# HAI Bundle Challenge
## English case bank for adult acute care

Each row contains a learner-facing scenario and question followed by the facilitator key. Reveal the key after the learner attempts the case. Cases 21–25 in each station are record audits. Use Done, Not done, Not documented, or Not applicable with a documented reason for individual audit items. For the calculation cases, divide fully verified opportunities by eligible opportunities. The order lists 18 November 2026 as its content review date.

## CLABSI — Central line-associated bloodstream infection

[WHO central venous catheter guideline (2026)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240121805) · [CDC intravascular catheter recommendations (2011)](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/intravascular-catheter-related-infections/summary-recommendations.html)

| Case | Learner scenario and question | Facilitator key |
|---|---|---|
| CLABSI-01 | In an adult oncology unit, the team is preparing to insert a central venous catheter. The sterile field includes only a small drape. What should be changed before insertion? | Add a full-body sterile drape as part of maximal sterile barrier precautions. |
| CLABSI-02 | The operator puts on sterile gloves before performing hand hygiene. What should the operator do before proceeding? | Remove the gloves, perform hand hygiene, and don a fresh pair of sterile gloves. |
| CLABSI-03 | The insertion site has been prepared with an alcohol-based antiseptic. The skin is still visibly wet when the operator is ready to puncture. What should happen next? | Allow the antiseptic to dry as directed by the manufacturer before puncture. |
| CLABSI-04 | Immediately before puncture, the team discovers a documented chlorhexidine allergy. How should the team proceed? | Use an approved alternative antiseptic appropriate for the site and allergy, following facility protocol. |
| CLABSI-05 | A trainee touches the prepared insertion site with a nonsterile finger while trying to locate the vein. What should the team do? | Pause, repeat skin preparation, and maintain aseptic technique before proceeding. |
| CLABSI-06 | The planned IV treatment requires one lumen. A team member requests a three-lumen catheter in case additional access is needed later. How many lumens should be selected? | Use the fewest lumens that meet the current treatment requirements. |
| CLABSI-07 | An adult with advanced kidney disease requires central venous access. The team proposes a subclavian line. What should be discussed before choosing the site? | Discuss an alternative site that preserves venous access for dialysis. |
| CLABSI-08 | A clinician without training in ultrasound-guided central line insertion plans to perform the procedure independently. How should the team respond? | Arrange for a qualified clinician trained in the technique to perform the insertion. |
| CLABSI-09 | A central line was placed during an emergency when aseptic technique could not be assured. The patient still requires central access. What is the plan for the line? | Arrange replacement as soon as possible and within 48 hours, per CDC. |
| CLABSI-10 | A nurse is about to connect an IV antibiotic to an exposed access port that has yet to be disinfected. What should the nurse do? | Disinfect the port per product instructions and connect using a sterile access device. |
| CLABSI-11 | A transparent central line dressing becomes wet during a shower. What should the nurse do during the dressing change? | Replace the damp dressing using aseptic technique and inspect the insertion site. |
| CLABSI-12 | During rounds, you notice that the dressing edge has lifted and the insertion site is exposed. What action is required? | Replace the dressing to restore intact sterile coverage. |
| CLABSI-13 | The insertion site continues to ooze immediately after placement. Which dressing is appropriate while the site is oozing? | Use sterile gauze until the oozing resolves, following facility protocol. |
| CLABSI-14 | A patient with a peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) asks whether they can soak the affected arm in a bath. What advice should you give? | Avoid submerging the catheter or site. Protect the site and connections during showering. |
| CLABSI-15 | A patient reports new tenderness beneath an opaque central line dressing. How should the site be assessed? | Remove the dressing using the appropriate technique and visually inspect the site. |
| CLABSI-16 | A functioning central line remains clinically necessary. A clinician proposes replacing it every week to prevent infection. What is the appropriate plan? | Continue indicated maintenance and review line necessity. Avoid routine scheduled replacement solely for infection prevention. |
| CLABSI-17 | A patient with a central line develops a fever. The site appears intact, and another source is possible. How should the team approach the removal decision? | Evaluate the patient and possible sources before deciding. Fever alone is insufficient grounds for routine removal. |
| CLABSI-18 | The team suspects infection involving a nontunneled central line and proposes exchanging it over a guidewire. How should you respond? | Avoid guidewire exchange of the suspected infected line and seek prompt clinical assessment. |
| CLABSI-19 | IV treatment has ended. The remaining medications can be given orally, and no other indication for the line is documented. What should the team do? | Coordinate prompt removal because central access is no longer needed. |
| CLABSI-20 | An oncology team member proposes routine topical antibiotic ointment at a PICC insertion site. What maintenance plan is appropriate? | Follow the approved PICC site-care protocol and avoid routine antibiotic ointment at this site. |
| CLABSI-21 | A signed insertion checklist marks maximal barriers complete. The observer saw the team use only a small sterile drape. How should this item be scored? | Not done. Record the observed barrier breach and reconcile the checklist discrepancy. |
| CLABSI-22 | The sole entry reads “Line care completed.” No one observed access, and the note gives no details about the port. How should port disinfection be classified? | Not documented. Seek verification before crediting the item. |
| CLABSI-23 | All applicable maintenance items were completed. The record confirms the line is still required for IV treatment. How should the round be scored? | Complete. The documented requirement supports keeping the line in place. |
| CLABSI-24 | A line removed yesterday still appears on today’s maintenance roster. How should it be handled in today’s audit? | Exclude it from today’s eligible opportunities and correct the roster. |
| CLABSI-25 | Nine of 12 eligible maintenance rounds met every applicable item. What was verified full compliance, and what should be reviewed? | 75% (9 ÷ 12). Review which items were missed in the other three rounds. |

## CAUTI — Catheter-associated urinary tract infection

[CDC CAUTI recommendations (2009)](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/cauti/summary-of-recommendations.html) · [CDC catheter clinical safety guidance (2025)](https://www.cdc.gov/uti/hcp/clinical-safety/)

| Case | Learner scenario and question | Facilitator key |
|---|---|---|
| CAUTI-01 | An older inpatient has urinary incontinence without retention. The ward team requests an indwelling catheter to simplify linen changes. How should the team manage the incontinence? | Use appropriate continence care and a toileting plan. Convenience alone does not justify catheterization. |
| CAUTI-02 | A cooperative male inpatient needs a urine collection device. He has no urinary retention or bladder outlet obstruction. Which option is worth assessing? | Assess suitability for an external urinary catheter. |
| CAUTI-03 | A critically ill adult needs accurate, ongoing urine output measurements to guide treatment. How should the catheter indication be managed? | Document the indication and review daily whether accurate measurement still requires an indwelling catheter. |
| CAUTI-04 | After surgery, a patient has yet to void. A bladder scanner is available. What should the team do before deciding on catheterization? | Assess bladder volume with the scanner according to facility protocol. |
| CAUTI-05 | The acute-care team prepares to insert an indwelling urinary catheter with clean examination gloves as the only gloves available. What setup is required? | Use aseptic insertion technique with sterile gloves and sterile equipment. |
| CAUTI-06 | A patient requires an indwelling catheter. A larger catheter is suggested without a clinical reason. What size criterion should guide the selection? | Choose the smallest bore that permits adequate drainage. |
| CAUTI-07 | During repositioning, the weight of the drainage tubing pulls on the catheter. What should be corrected? | Secure the catheter to limit movement and prevent traction. |
| CAUTI-08 | After a bed transfer, you find the drainage bag hanging above the patient’s bladder. Where should it be positioned? | Rehang the bag below bladder level. |
| CAUTI-09 | The drainage bag hangs below the bladder but touches the floor. How should the setup be adjusted? | Keep the bag below bladder level and suspend it clear of the floor. |
| CAUTI-10 | The drainage bag is below the bladder, but urine is no longer reaching it. You find a kink where the tubing passes under the patient’s thigh. What should you do first? | Release the kink and reassess urine flow. |
| CAUTI-11 | During care, the catheter inadvertently disconnects from the collection tubing. What should happen to the drainage system? | Replace the catheter and collection system using aseptic technique and sterile equipment. |
| CAUTI-12 | Urine is leaking from a cracked connector between the catheter and collection tubing. What should the nurse do? | Replace the catheter and damaged collection system using aseptic technique and sterile equipment. |
| CAUTI-13 | A urine culture is ordered for a patient with an indwelling catheter. Urine is present in the drainage bag. Where and how should the sample be obtained? | Disinfect the sampling port and obtain a fresh specimen aseptically through it. |
| CAUTI-14 | On rounds, you see the same emptying container being used for the drainage bags of two patients. What should change? | Provide a separate clean emptying container for each patient. |
| CAUTI-15 | While the bag is being emptied, the drain spigot touches the receiving container. What should the nurse do? | Prevent further contact and follow the facility’s procedure for managing the contamination. |
| CAUTI-16 | During daily bathing, a staff member suggests using an antiseptic around the urethral meatus to prevent CAUTI. What care is appropriate? | Provide routine hygiene during daily bathing. Routine antiseptic cleansing for CAUTI prevention is unnecessary. |
| CAUTI-17 | An indwelling catheter is functioning normally. A weekly exchange is requested without a clinical indication. What should guide replacement? | Use clinical indications such as obstruction or a compromised closed system to guide replacement. |
| CAUTI-18 | A clinician proposes bladder irrigation with an antimicrobial solely to prevent CAUTI. How should the team respond? | Avoid routine antimicrobial bladder irrigation for prevention. |
| CAUTI-19 | It is the first postoperative day, and the reason for the urinary catheter has resolved. What should happen next? | Remove it promptly, preferably within 24 hours after surgery when no indication remains. |
| CAUTI-20 | Before planned removal, a team member suggests clamping the catheter for several hours to train the bladder. What is the appropriate plan? | Follow the removal protocol. Routine pre-removal clamping is unnecessary. |
| CAUTI-21 | You observed both a bag above bladder level and a kink in the tubing. The bag has since been lowered, but the tubing is still kinked. How should the correction be scored, and what remains? | Credit bag position. Clear the kink and reassess flow before marking drainage complete. If both corrections are presented together, accept either order. |
| CAUTI-22 | You observe prompt catheter removal today after the indication resolves. Yesterday’s chart has no record of a necessity review. How should the two items be scored? | Mark today’s removal Done and yesterday’s review Not documented. |
| CAUTI-23 | The drainage system is closed and the bag is below the bladder. The team confirms the catheter remains solely for staff convenience. Is the bundle complete? | Incomplete. Reassess the indication and coordinate removal if there is no clinical need. |
| CAUTI-24 | A patient on the maintenance audit roster uses a handheld urinal and has no indwelling catheter. Should this record count? | Exclude the record from the indwelling-catheter maintenance denominator. |
| CAUTI-25 | Of 20 eligible opportunities, 16 met every element, three had an observed missed step, and one lacked documentation needed to verify completion. What is the result? | Verified full compliance is 80% (16 ÷ 20). Report the documentation gap separately from the three observed misses. |

## VAP — Ventilator-associated pneumonia

[SHEA and partner organizations’ acute-care VAP/VAE prevention update (2022)](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/150381)

| Case | Learner scenario and question | Facilitator key |
|---|---|---|
| VAP-01 | An adult receiving invasive mechanical ventilation is lying flat. There is no contraindication to head-of-bed elevation. What adjustment is indicated? | Elevate the head of the bed to 30–45°. |
| VAP-02 | A temporary order restricts head-of-bed elevation after a procedure. The restriction is still active. How should the team manage positioning? | Follow the order and document the reason and planned reassessment time. |
| VAP-03 | The temporary positioning restriction has ended, but the patient remains flat. What should the nurse do now? | Reassess positioning and elevate the head of the bed if it is safe to do so. |
| VAP-04 | After transport, a ventilated patient returns to the ICU lying flat. There is no ongoing positioning restriction. What should the receiving team check? | Check the head-of-bed angle and restore the indicated elevation. |
| VAP-05 | The oral-care plan for a ventilated adult with natural teeth consists only of moistening the lips. What should be added? | Include daily toothbrushing and appropriate oral care. |
| VAP-06 | The order set includes oral chlorhexidine by default for every ventilated adult. What should routine VAP-prevention oral care include? | Provide oral care with toothbrushing and omit routine chlorhexidine for VAP prevention. |
| VAP-07 | A ventilated patient remains deeply sedated. The chart contains neither a target nor a current reason for deep sedation. What should the team review? | Agree on a sedation target and use the lightest level appropriate for the patient. |
| VAP-08 | A spontaneous awakening trial is on today’s plan, but a contraindication is documented. What should the team do? | Apply the eligibility screen, defer the trial, and document the contraindication. |
| VAP-09 | A stable ventilated patient meets the unit’s criteria for a spontaneous awakening trial. What should happen next? | Coordinate the trial and monitor tolerance according to unit protocol. |
| VAP-10 | An awake patient receiving invasive ventilation meets the readiness criteria for a spontaneous breathing trial. What is the next step? | Coordinate a spontaneous breathing trial with the care team. |
| VAP-11 | During a spontaneous breathing trial, the patient meets the unit’s criteria for trial intolerance. How should the team respond? | Stop the trial according to protocol and reassess the cause with the team. |
| VAP-12 | A patient completes a spontaneous breathing trial successfully. A team member suggests proceeding immediately to extubation on that basis alone. What remains to be assessed? | Confirm extubation readiness, including the ability to protect the airway. |
| VAP-13 | A stable patient has remained in bed for several days despite meeting the unit’s mobility criteria. What should the team plan? | Plan early mobilization at a level appropriate to the patient’s abilities. |
| VAP-14 | During a mobility assessment, the team identifies a temporary contraindication to the planned activity. How should the plan be updated? | Adjust the activity to what is safe and specify when eligibility will be reassessed. |
| VAP-15 | The ventilator circuit is intact and visibly clean. The shift checklist calls for a daily change. What should guide circuit replacement? | Replace the circuit when visibly soiled, malfunctioning, or required by manufacturer instructions. |
| VAP-16 | Visible contamination is found in the breathing circuit. What action is appropriate? | Replace the circuit according to facility protocol and device instructions. |
| VAP-17 | A mechanical circuit fault is compromising ventilation. What are the immediate priorities? | Ensure effective ventilation and coordinate replacement of the faulty circuit. |
| VAP-18 | A ventilated adult has a functioning gastrointestinal tract and is eligible for enteral feeding. Which approach should the team favor? | Favor early enteral nutrition according to the clinical plan. |
| VAP-19 | A ventilated patient has a high aspiration risk and is unable to tolerate gastric feeding. What feeding option should the team discuss? | Discuss whether postpyloric feeding is appropriate. |
| VAP-20 | Before intubation, the team expects the adult patient to require ventilation for more than 48–72 hours. What additional measure could be considered? | Consider an endotracheal tube with subglottic secretion drainage, depending on resources and facility protocol. |
| VAP-21 | A spontaneous awakening trial was deferred for a documented contraindication. All other elements were completed. The audit protocol permits this exemption. How should the opportunity be scored? | Mark the trial Not applicable with the documented reason. Count the opportunity as complete for its applicable elements. |
| VAP-22 | The head of the bed is below target. The audit form lists “medical contraindication,” but the record gives no supporting reason. How should this be handled? | Withhold the exemption pending verification and correct the position if it is safe. |
| VAP-23 | The toothbrushing field records only that mouth moisture was provided to a patient with natural teeth. How should toothbrushing be scored? | Toothbrushing is unverified. Check what care was performed before crediting it. |
| VAP-24 | A patient was extubated yesterday and has left the ICU. The record still appears on today’s invasive-ventilation audit list. How should it be handled? | Exclude today’s record from the eligible denominator for this bundle. |
| VAP-25 | Of six eligible rounds, four met every applicable element and two were incomplete. Calculate verified full compliance. | 66.7% (4 ÷ 6, rounded to one decimal place). This percentage measures completion of the audited elements. |

## SSI — Surgical site infection

[WHO global SSI prevention guideline, second edition (2018)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241550475) · [CDC SSI prevention guideline (2017)](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/surgical-site-infection/)

| Case | Learner scenario and question | Facilitator key |
|---|---|---|
| SSI-01 | During preparation for elective surgery, a patient asks whether she should bathe before admission. What advice should the preoperative nurse give? | Advise preoperative bathing or showering according to facility instructions. |
| SSI-02 | Hair is present at the proposed incision site, but it will not interfere with the operation. How should the team handle it? | Leave the hair in place. |
| SSI-03 | Hair interferes with access to the planned incision. The preparation trolley contains a razor and surgical clippers. Which method should the team use? | Use surgical clippers when hair removal is necessary. |
| SSI-04 | A patient shaved the operative site before arrival. On assessment, the nurse sees superficial scratches. What should happen before skin preparation? | Alert the team for assessment of the skin and avoid further shaving. |
| SSI-05 | An adult is scheduled for elective colorectal surgery with an indication for antimicrobial prophylaxis. The order is present, but administration has yet to be confirmed. What should the team verify before incision? | Confirm actual administration and timing against the applicable prophylaxis protocol. |
| SSI-06 | For a planned operation, the team proposes giving the first indicated prophylactic antibiotic dose after incision. How should the plan be adjusted? | Schedule administration before incision so adequate antimicrobial concentrations are present at incision. |
| SSI-07 | The selected prophylactic antimicrobial requires a longer infusion. How should the team schedule it relative to incision? | Plan the infusion start using the agent-specific timing and facility protocol. |
| SSI-08 | Prophylaxis has been administered, but a prolonged delay pushes back the operation. What should the team reassess? | Review coverage and dose timing with anesthesia and pharmacy under the agent-specific protocol. |
| SSI-09 | An operation continues beyond the redosing interval specified in the antimicrobial protocol. What should the team do? | Coordinate redosing under the protocol and document administration time. |
| SSI-10 | A clean-contaminated operation has ended and the incision is closed. The patient has no infection that requires treatment. What is the plan for prophylaxis? | End surgical prophylaxis at closure, consistent with the CDC recommendation for clean and clean-contaminated procedures. |
| SSI-11 | A drain remains after a clean operation. A team member proposes continuing prophylactic antibiotics until drain removal. How should the team respond? | End prophylaxis as indicated. Drain presence alone does not justify extending it. |
| SSI-12 | A patient has an established infection and a separate postoperative antibiotic treatment order. How should the team manage treatment when prophylaxis ends? | Review the therapeutic antibiotic plan independently with the treating team. |
| SSI-13 | An adult with intact skin is undergoing surgery and has no contraindication to the planned alcohol-based skin preparation. Which preparation does WHO recommend? | Use a suitable alcohol-based chlorhexidine preparation according to product and facility instructions. |
| SSI-14 | Before skin preparation, the nurse identifies a documented allergy to the usual antiseptic. What should the team choose? | Choose an approved alternative compatible with the surgical site and documented allergy. |
| SSI-15 | The product on the preparation tray is unsuitable for the operative site according to its labeling. What should you do before applying any antiseptic? | Check the exact operative site and product instructions, then select a suitable approved alternative. |
| SSI-16 | Intraoperative monitoring shows the patient’s temperature falling. What action should the team take? | Continue temperature monitoring and use appropriate measures to maintain normothermia. |
| SSI-17 | A patient with no known diabetes develops elevated glucose around the time of surgery. What should the team do? | Assess and manage glucose according to the facility’s perioperative protocol. |
| SSI-18 | After incision closure, a clinician proposes applying antimicrobial powder to the incision as routine SSI prophylaxis. How should the team respond? | Avoid routine application of antimicrobial powder to the incision for SSI prevention. |
| SSI-19 | A team member proposes completing surgical hand antisepsis after putting on sterile gloves. What is the correct sequence? | Perform surgical hand preparation before donning sterile gloves. |
| SSI-20 | An adult is scheduled for elective colorectal surgery. The proposed bowel preparation consists of mechanical preparation alone. What should the team review? | Review the colorectal protocol. WHO suggests oral antibiotics combined with mechanical bowel preparation in addition to standard IV prophylaxis. |
| SSI-21 | The prophylactic antibiotic was ordered at 07:00 and the incision was made at 08:00. There is no recorded administration time. How should the timing item be scored? | Not documented. Verify actual administration in the medication record before crediting timing. |
| SSI-22 | The operative record confirms that hair was left in place because it did not interfere with surgery. How should the hair-removal method item be scored? | Accept leaving the hair in place. Mark the removal method Not applicable and record the reason. |
| SSI-23 | The operative record documents an exception to the usual antiseptic and confirms use of a protocol-approved alternative suitable for the site. How should the preparation be scored? | Credit the documented preparation with the appropriate alternative. |
| SSI-24 | The prophylaxis checklist is signed complete. Direct observation confirms the indicated first dose was given after incision. How should the timing item be scored? | Not done. Reconcile the discrepancy and review the reason for the delay. |
| SSI-25 | Of 10 eligible operations, eight met all audit elements. Each of the other two missed a different item. What should the audit report? | Full compliance is 80% (8 ÷ 10). Report item-level results to show the two distinct gaps. |

# Antigravity addendum — The Handover Implementation Order

Append this to the IPC Bundle Scenario Cards implementation order. Implement The Handover as a practice mode inside the existing module, launched from its deck selector. Keep the existing gallery layout and student access rules. Use English for case text, voice scripts, captions, and feedback.

## Round flow

1. **Receive handover.** Show the two-sentence handover with optional audio. Give the learner a replay button and a visible transcript.
2. **Inspect evidence.** Let the learner examine the relevant bedside equipment and chart entries in either order. On phones, provide Room and Chart tabs and keep the current question visible.
3. **Act.** Let the learner correct a scene, verify a chart entry, or record an audit finding. Use clear action labels and give immediate feedback on each correction.
4. **Explain.** Ask the learner to identify the evidence that supports the decision. Show the keyed explanation and local bundle reference after submission.
5. **Hand off.** Ask “What did you see?”, “What did you do?”, and “What would you tell the next shift?” Offer a model response after the learner submits or skips. Keep the reflection separate from first-attempt scoring.

## Playable starter cards

Verify every key against the corresponding facility form before enabling a card. Add the exact document and checklist row to its record. Where the form is unavailable or unclear, leave the card in trainer preview with a source-review flag. Use the prior implementation order to extend this mode across all seven decks.

### HO-CAUTI-01 — The transfer

- **Facility Form Reference:** Form CAUTI-F-02: Adult Inpatient Nursing Flowsheet & Catheter Maintenance Bundle, Row 3: Dependent Drainage & Bag Position (Below bladder level, unkinked).
- **Voice handover.** “The patient has just returned to the ward. Please check the urinary drainage setup after the transfer.”
- **Room.** The collection bag hangs above bladder level. Traceable tubing is kinked under the thigh. Use a side view that shows both clues clearly.
- **Question.** “Correct the drainage setup.”
- **Actions and feedback.** Lowering the bag updates its position and displays “The bag is below bladder level. Check the tubing.” Clearing the kink straightens the tubing and displays “The tubing is clear. Check the bag position.” If the other correction is already complete, show “Both drainage issues are corrected. Reassess flow.” Accept either order and update only the affected equipment.
- **Model handoff.** “After transfer, the bag was above bladder level and the tubing was kinked. I corrected both and reassessed drainage.”

### HO-CVC-01 — The signed checklist

- **Facility Form Reference:** Form IPC-CL-04: Central Venous Catheter Insertion Safety Checklist, Row 4: Maximal Sterile Barrier Precautions (Cap, Mask, Sterile Gown, Sterile Gloves, Full-Body Drape).
- **Voice handover.** “The team is preparing for central line insertion. The signed checklist marks the sterile barrier setup complete.”
- **Room and chart.** The learner sees the checklist entry and a realistic setup showing only a small drape. Other visible equipment should match the approved insertion setup.
- **Question.** “Is the documented barrier setup supported by what you see? Select the observation and corrective action.”
- **Key.** Mark the barrier item Not done, record the discrepancy, and add the full-body sterile drape before insertion.
- **Model handoff.** “The checklist was signed, but the setup showed only a small drape. I flagged the discrepancy and requested the full-body sterile drape before insertion.”

### HO-SSI-01 — Three records

- **Facility Form Reference:** Form OR-SSI-01: Surgical Antimicrobial Prophylaxis Audit Form, Row 2: Pre-incision Antibiotic Prophylaxis Timing Verification.
- **Voice handover.** “We’re reviewing surgical prophylaxis after the operation. The medication order and incision time are available.”
- **Chart.** Show three records the learner can open independently. Order placed at 07:00. Incision at 08:00. Administration time is missing.
- **Question.** “Can you verify prophylaxis timing? Record the audit finding and choose the next step.”
- **Key.** Mark timing Not documented and seek the actual administration record. Feedback should explain the difference between order and administration times.
- **Replay variant HO-SSI-02.** Keep the same room and chart layout. Change the administration record to 08:10. If pre-incision prophylaxis was indicated under the local protocol, the key is Not done and the timing discrepancy should be reviewed.
- **Model handoff.** “I could confirm the order and incision times. I could not verify when the dose was given, so I flagged the missing administration time.”

### HO-VAP-01 — Return later

- **Facility Form Reference:** Form ICU-VENT-01: Mechanical Ventilation Care Bundle Daily Flowsheet, Row 1: Head of Bed Elevation (30°–45° unless contraindicated/restricted).
- **Voice handover.** “The patient has a temporary positioning restriction. Please check the order before adjusting the bed.”
- **First visit.** The bed is flat and the chart shows an active restriction with a reassessment time. The learner should follow the restriction and record when to reassess.
- **Second visit.** Advance to the documented reassessment time through a “Return later” button. Show that the restriction has ended. The learner reassesses safety and, if appropriate, restores the head-of-bed elevation specified by the local bundle.
- **Model handoff.** “The bed stayed flat while the restriction was active. I reassessed after it ended and adjusted the position when it was safe.”

## Production and acceptance

- Use interactive, photorealistic bedside scenes with medically accurate props and readable chart records. Give each equipment control an equivalent keyboard action with a visible focus indicator.
- Use subtle room tone, a soft hardware click for equipment adjustments, and a brief completion cue. Play audio after user interaction. Supply captions, mute, and volume controls. All clues must remain available with sound muted. Use original or appropriately licensed assets and record provenance.
- Provide solo play and an optional instructor-led pair setup. Give one learner the room evidence and the other the chart evidence, then ask them to give a joint handover. Use two facilitator views or printable role cards if the repository lacks synchronized multiplayer infrastructure.
- Record first-attempt results independently of retries. Save after each scene correction and restore the exact state after refresh. In replay, change only the clue that changes the decision.
- Verify both CAUTI correction orders, refresh after the first correction, the two SSI evidence variants, and the two VAP visits. Test with sound muted, keyboard only, and a narrow phone viewport. Confirm the new mode is reachable from the existing student route and that source-review drafts are excluded from student practice.
